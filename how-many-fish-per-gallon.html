How many fish per gallon?

The inch rule's failures, this calculator's point classes, and a 10-gallon walkthrough.

Quick answer: The old 1-inch-per-gallon rule breaks because waste scales with body mass, not length. This calculator uses small fish 1 point, medium 3, large 8, and XL 14. Run it with adult size.

Where 1-inch-per-gallon breaks

Direction one: a 10-inch oscar "fits" a 10-gallon by the inch rule and has no room to turn. Direction two: twelve small fish in 10 gallons are 12 points. Capacity on a normal setup is 10, so this calculator calls that 120% and overstocked. Length is not the load.

Calculator walkthrough for a 10-gallon tank

Section added October 6, 2026. This block is the stocking function, inputs shown. Enter them on the stocking calculator. Points are a planning estimate for a cycled freshwater tank, not a published standard.

10 gallons, normal setup, 8 small fish: 8 points, capacity 10, 80%, comfortable. Ten small fish are 100%, at capacity. Eleven are 110%, overstocked.

Capacity is round(10 × 1.0) = 10. Eight fish are 8 × 1 = 8 points, and round(8 / 10 × 100) = 80. Comfortable means the percent is at or under 80, so 80 is the last comfortable step on this function. Ten fish are round(10 / 10 × 100) = 100, which is at capacity (above 80 and at or under 100). Eleven fish are round(11 / 10 × 100) = 110, overstocked. FAQ

What does 8 small fish score in a 10-gallon tank?

8 points against a capacity of 10, which is 80%, and the status is comfortable because 80 is the top of that band. Eleven small fish are 11 points, 110%, and overstocked. Ten small fish are 10 points, 100%, at capacity.

Is that score a published stocking standard?

No. Points, watts, and turnover on this site are planning estimates for a cycled freshwater tank. The 10-gallon arithmetic is the calculator function with the inputs shown. It is not a university headcount.

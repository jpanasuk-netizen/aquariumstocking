What Size Tank Do Zebra Danios Need?

Adult standard length 2.0 inches. Active swimmers: a group of at least 8-10 needs a 36 × 12 inch base, about 21 US gallons.

Quick answer: Zebra danios (Danio rerio) need a minimum base of 36 × 12 inches, about 21 US gallons, for an active group of at least 8-10, at an adult standard length of 2.0 inches (Seriously Fish). Filter at 325-405 liters per hour (85.87-107.00 gallons per hour) and keep 64-77°F. The profile lists a 50 watt heater, while Aqueon places 21 gallons in the 100 watt band (15-40 gallons), just past the 50 watt band that ends at 20.

How to size it

Length is the point of this minimum. Seriously Fish calls zebra danios active and asks for a group of at least 8-10, on a 36 × 12 inch base. That 36 inch run is longer than the 24 inch base used for neon tetras, platies, cherry barbs, and harlequin rasboras, even though the adult fish is only 2.0 inches. About 21 US gallons is the volume that goes with the longer base. It is not the 31 inch base used for bronze corys.

325 ÷ 3.785 = 85.87 gph. 405 ÷ 3.785 = 107.00 gph.

Four times about 21 gallons is 84 gallons per hour, and five times is 105. The converted rating, 85.87-107.00 gph, follows that four-to-five turnover. Flow is not marked gentle.

The heater is where the two sources part. The profile lists 64-77°F and 50 watts, assuming a room around 68°F. Aqueon gives 50 watts to tanks of 5-20 gallons. About 21 is above 20, so it misses that band. It falls in the 100 watt band, 15-40 gallons, and it is short of the 150 watt band, which starts at 30. Read both: the species page says 50 watts, and the volume chart says a 21-gallon aquarium is a 100 watt tank. This page does not average 50 and 100 into a new wattage.

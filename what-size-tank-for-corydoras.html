What Size Tank Does a Bronze Cory Need?

Adult standard length 3.0 inches. Keep a group of at least 4-6 on a 31 × 12 inch base, about 19 US gallons.

Quick answer: A bronze cory (Corydoras aeneus) needs a minimum base of 31 × 12 inches, about 19 US gallons, and a group of at least 4-6, at an adult standard length of 3.0 inches (Seriously Fish). The filter is 290-360 liters per hour (76.62-95.11 gallons per hour), and the temperature is 70-81°F with a 50-75 watt heater. About 19 gallons sits in two Aqueon bands at once: 50 watts for 5-20 gallons and 100 watts for 15-40 gallons.

How to size it

The base is the shopping clue. Seriously Fish asks for 31 × 12 inches, about 19 US gallons, for a bottom group of at least 4-6. A 31 inch length is the case the 20 gallon long search is meant to find. About 19 US gallons is the volume to judge it by, not a guess that a taller tank of the same gallons would do. Adult standard length is 3.0 inches, which is the fish, not the tank.

290 ÷ 3.785 = 76.62 gph. 360 ÷ 3.785 = 95.11 gph.

Four times about 19 gallons is 76 gallons per hour, and five times is 95. The converted rating, 76.62-95.11 gph, matches that turnover almost gallon for gallon. The profile does not call the flow gentle. Bristlenose plecos share this 290-360 liter rating and this 31 × 12 inch base; they do not share the cory temperature or the 4-6 group count.

About 19 gallons is inside two Aqueon lines. Aqueon gives 50 watts to 5-20 gallons and 100 watts to 15-40 gallons. 19 is not above 20, and it is not below 15, so both bands include it. The 150 watt band starts at 30 and does not. The profile's heater is 50-75 watts for 70-81°F, assuming a room around 68°F. 75 watts sits between the 50 watt and 100 watt Aqueon ratings; it is not a third band on the Aqueon chart.

What Size Tank Do Mollies Need?

Adult standard length 3.1 inches. The published tank is 36 × 12 × 12 inches, about 21 US gallons.

Quick answer: Mollies (Poecilia sphenops) need a tank of 36 × 12 × 12 inches, about 21 US gallons, at an adult standard length of 3.1 inches, and no group count is printed in the figures used here (Seriously Fish). Filter at 325-405 liters per hour (85.87-107.00 gallons per hour) and keep 70-82°F with a 75-100 watt heater. Aqueon puts about 21 gallons on the 100 watt band (15-40 gallons), past the 50 watt band that ends at 20.

How to size it

The floor matches the zebra danio minimum, and the molly profile adds a 12 inch height: 36 × 12 × 12 inches, about 21 US gallons (Seriously Fish). The fish is larger than a danio. Adult standard length is 3.1 inches, against the danio's 2.0 inches. No group count is printed here, while the danio profile asks for at least 8-10. Do not copy the danio headcount onto mollies.

325 ÷ 3.785 = 85.87 gph. 405 ÷ 3.785 = 107.00 gph. Same liter box as the zebra danio.

Four times about 21 gallons is 84 gallons per hour, and five times is 105. The converted 85.87-107.00 gph follows that rule. Flow is not marked gentle.

Heat is where mollies line up with the volume chart and danios do not. The profile lists 70-82°F and 75-100 watts, assuming a room around 68°F. Aqueon lists 100 watts for 15-40 gallons. About 21 is inside that band. It is above 20, so it is outside the 50 watt band, and it is below 30, so it is outside the 150 watt band. 100 watts is the shared figure: the top of the profile's 75-100 watt span and the Aqueon rating for this volume.

FAQ

What size tank do mollies need?

Seriously Fish lists 36 × 12 × 12 inches, about 21 US gallons, for Poecilia sphenops. Adult standard length is 3.1 inches. The profile figures used here do not include a group count.

What filter rating is listed for mollies?

Seriously Fish lists 325-405 liters per hour for Poecilia sphenops on the 36 × 12 × 12 inch tank. Dividing by 3.785 gives 85.87-107.00 gallons per hour. Four times about 21 gallons is 84 gallons per hour, and five times is 105.

What heater wattage matches about 21 gallons for mollies?

Seriously Fish lists 70-82°F and a 75-100 watt heater, assuming a room around 68°F. Aqueon lists 100 watts for 15-40 gallons, which includes about 21 gallons, and 50 watts for 5-20 gallons, which does not.

What Size Tank Do Cherry Barbs Need?

Adult standard length 2.0 inches. Schooling fish: at least 6-10 on a 24 × 12 inch base, about 14 US gallons.

Full breakdown: how many fish per gallon. Full breakdown: how many fish in a 20 gallon tank.

Quick answer: Cherry barbs (Puntius titteya) need a minimum base of 24 × 12 inches, about 14 US gallons, and Seriously Fish says they are schooling fish kept in a group of at least 6-10, at an adult standard length of 2.0 inches. Filter at 215-270 liters per hour (56.80-71.33 gallons per hour) and keep 68-81°F with a 50-75 watt heater. Aqueon lists 50 watts for 5-20 gallons, which contains about 14, and the 100 watt band does not start until 15.

How to size it

The school is slightly smaller than the neon and harlequin groups, and that is a published difference, not a style choice. Seriously Fish says at least 6-10, on 24 × 12 inches, about 14 US gallons. Neon tetras and harlequin rasboras are at least 8-10 on that same base. Adult standard length is 2.0 inches, the same length printed for zebra danios and platies, on a shorter tank than the danio's 36 inches.

215 ÷ 3.785 = 56.80 gph. 270 ÷ 3.785 = 71.33 gph.

The liter box matches those other 24 × 12 inch species that are not marked for gentle flow. Four times about 14 gallons is 56 gallons per hour and five times is 70, and 56.80-71.33 gph sits on that band. A ram on the same footprint is the gentle-flow exception (29.06-42.27 gph), so the cherry barb filter should not be copied from the ram.

The heater span is 50-75 watts for 68-81°F, assuming a room around 68°F. Aqueon only has the 50 watt band under this volume: 5-20 gallons contains 14, and 15-40 does not, because 14 is below 15. 75 watts is inside the species span and is not a separate Aqueon row. The 100 watt row starts one gallon higher than this minimum.
